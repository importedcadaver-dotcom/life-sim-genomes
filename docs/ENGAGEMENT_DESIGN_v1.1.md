# v1.1: Rewarding curiosity without manufacturing compulsion

The request included research on addictive game-design methods. Research on video-game engagement distinguishes intrinsically satisfying autonomy and competence from deceptive or exploitative retention practices. We therefore used **self-directed exploration, legible discovery, positive feedback and optional challenges**, rather than deliberately inducing loss of control.

## Implemented

- **Genome Detective** joins Berry Hunt and Speed Detective under `E`. It asks which of three real specimens has the largest inherited regulatory program. Answers reveal measured values. The original world is never edited and its RNG is never consumed by the minigame.
- **New evidence-based milestones** appear in the field notes: inherited novel regulatory gene and newly formed chromosome. Notifications are generated only from real counters, not random or scripted "discoveries."
- **Inspect the surprising result:** `G` opens DNA; `VIEW REGULATORS` reveals chromosomes, innovation IDs, noncoding genes, signal connections and current expression. A specimen’s genome is persisted after death.
- **Visible choices without hidden consequences:** cheat interventions are recorded, laboratory A/B runs do not modify the main world, and challenge stars do not change fitness.
- **No purchase system, daily login streaks, punishments for leaving, urgency counters, deceptive drop rates, dark-pattern prompts or paid randomized rewards.**

The core engagement loop is **notice → investigate → predict → compare → document**, and pausing or stepping time is always under player control. Future opportunities include pinned hypotheses, accessible gene-change diffs, transparent science goals, and player-created ecology challenges. Progress should measure scientific discovery and player comprehension, *not* session length.
