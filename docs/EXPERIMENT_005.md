# Experiment 005 — Search versus residence (v0.5)

**Status:** Completed exploratory follow-up; 2026-11-02. Raw outcomes and analysis code included. **These results do not demonstrate intelligence, planning or purposeful long-range navigation.**

## Why this experiment exists

Experiment 004 showed apparently contradictory behavior: evolved steering improves food harvesting in a sparse landscape, yet disabling turning makes agents enter isolated food cores more often. The most parsimonious explanation to test is a *search-versus-residence tradeoff*: fast, straight movers intersect patches, while turning organisms spend longer exploiting patches after encounter.

This is a **post-hoc mechanism test** using already selected v0.4 genomes, not new training. If food sensing is removed, and residency remains high, that would weaken any claim of resource-specific steering.

## Protocol

- **Independent training replicates:** eight archived training seeds 701–708. Populations are sampled from founder genomes and three sets of existing checkpoints (`dense`, `islands`, `islands_costly`). Each condition is evaluated once per training seed; **individual organisms are not treated as eight times 36 statistically independent populations**.
- **Environments:** 96×96 reflecting-wall square with 36 stationary Gaussian food cores on a 6×6 grid, radius/width of food hills 2.35, contact radius exactly 3.0 units. For each trial, 36 sampled controllers are placed in the same matched positions and headings.
- **Search assay:** each organism begins **6.15 units from a food core**, heading randomly. Record ever entered any core, time to entry, cumulative fraction of time in a core, maximum uninterrupted residence streak, and harvested food before/after the first entry.
- **Residence assay:** each organism begins **1.1 units inside a core**. Record time spent inside any core, unbroken ten-tick initial stay, exits, returns, maximum streak, and harvest.
- **Four causal interventions:** natural inherited turning at externally fixed forward speed **0.60**; completely suppressed turn; food-sensor blindness (motor controller retains other inputs); and left/right food-cue inversion. Identical environmental seeds per comparison. No reproduction or mutation in the assays; adequate initial energy prevents death; 75 steps each.
- **Counts:** 8 training seeds × 4 genome groups × 2 starting positions × 4 motor conditions = **256 trials**, each involving 36 probes (9,216 organism-trajectories). This last number is **not** the independent sample size.
- **Statistics:** pair within training seed; descriptive eight-seed bootstrap percentile intervals generated by the existing `paired_ci()` function (4,000 bootstrap draws). All contrasts exploratory, no multiple-testing adjustment.

## Results: trained in costly sparse islands

| Quantity | Evolved turning | No turning | Direction of difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Search: entered a food core at least once | **0.337** | 0.743 | Straight motion finds cores more often |
| Search: total time inside a core | **0.137** | 0.105 | Turning slightly improves aggregate occupancy |
| Search: food harvested per organism per step | **0.537** | 0.482 | Turning slightly improves food collected |
| Residence: total time inside a core | **0.809** | 0.147 | Turning makes local residence far more persistent |
| Residence: food harvested per organism per step | **1.351** | 0.543 | Remaining local makes agents much more productive |
| Residence: unbroken 10-step stay | **0.566** | 0.000 | Straight-moving agents leave rapidly |

Across eight training seeds, the **normal-turn minus zero-turn residence advantage** was **+0.6615** in occupancy fraction (descriptive bootstrap 95% interval **[0.6106, 0.7131]**), positive in 8/8 seeds. The corresponding harvest advantage was **+0.8084** units per agent-step (interval **[0.7709, 0.8427]**), positive 8/8. Normal steering **reduced search acquisition** relative to straight trajectories by 0.4062 (interval **[-0.4722, -0.3229]**), in 8/8 seeds (normal was lower all eight).

The other three genome groups also showed strong positive residence-minus-no-turn differences. With zero-turn and fixed-speed, motor commands are identical regardless of the inherited genome; so the constant control values across groups are expected and confirm the causal intervention isolates steering.

## What selection changed

In matched residence assays, **costly-island-trained controllers** occupied core regions on average **0.809**, compared with **0.645** for unevolved founders under the same speed clamp (+0.1634, positive in 8/8 training seeds; descriptive interval **[0.1083, 0.2141]**). Food harvesting improved from 1.204 to 1.351 (+0.1471, 8/8 positive; interval **[0.1120, 0.1831]**).

With search starts, costly-island-trained controllers entered a core 0.337 of the time vs founder controllers 0.260 (+0.0764, 7/8 positive). Yet *both groups still entered far fewer cores than straight movers*. The most robust acquired change is **retention/exploitation**, not efficient long-range patch acquisition.

## Most important negative control

**Food-sensor blindness did not abolish retention.** Costly-island-trained controllers with food cues disabled spent **0.830** of their time inside food cores, slightly *more* than the normal controller's **0.809**. The result is inconsistent with a simple story in which directional food sensing is necessary to keep these agents localized. It is consistent with genetically evolved turning or looping behavior that keeps them near where they began, even without knowing whether food is there. Other nonfood sensory inputs (energy and crowding) remain active, so this is not a complete sensor-free ablation.

It remains possible to evolve accurate food-guided acquisition with different sensory architecture, locomotion, resource topology, ecological selection pressure, or lifespans. This experiment has not demonstrated it.

## Methodological caveats

1. Genomes come from existing v0.4 checkpoints. The new protocol was motivated by those results and is **not preregistered independent confirmation**.
2. The two assays deliberately differ in starting distance. This isolates behaviors but is not a complete lifetime simulation or an evaluation of reproductive fitness.
3. Core assignment is based on proximity to **any** target, not recognition of a goal or home patch.
4. Contact and residence thresholds were specified for this test; other thresholds might change magnitudes.
5. In the residency assay, arrivals are initialized rather than learned. A long residence can be a passive turning pattern, not deliberation.
6. A matched zero-turn agent has its genotype bypassed completely under the externally fixed-speed override. It is a motor-mechanism baseline, not an independently evolving lineage.
7. Multiple genome and outcome comparisons were explored. Bootstrap intervals describe variation over only eight training seeds and are not confirmatory significance claims.
8. We should separately test motor trajectories in **barren, food-free** patches to see whether the controller loops in empty space. We should also test entry/residence across larger and smaller cores.

## Scientific conclusion

**The available controllers exhibit a search/residence tradeoff, and selection under sparse/high-cost conditions favored stronger local residence. There is no evidence yet that resource-specific sensing is the mechanism of that residence.** The central question for Experiment 006 is whether persistent turning arises from a generic genetic motor bias or a heritable conditional response to a perceptible environmental signal.
